Houston Travel Guide: Food, Culture, Attractions, and Nightlife

Overview
Houston, Texas is known for food, culture, diversity, museums, shopping, nightlife, and large city attractions. The city is one of the most ethnically diverse places in the world and has a strong reputation for global cuisine and cultural experiences.

Fun Fact
Houston’s official nickname, “Space City,” comes from its historic role as a major center for NASA activity.

Transportation
Houston is a large and spread-out city, so renting a car is strongly recommended. Driving is likely cheaper and more convenient than relying on Uber or Lyft for every stop.

What To Do in Houston

Museum of Fine Arts, Houston
The Museum of Fine Arts is home to a variety of art collections and exhibitions. It is one of Houston’s major cultural attractions.

Admission information from the source:
- Adults: $19.
- College students and youth: $12.
- Children under 12: free.
- Free admission is available for all visitors on Thursdays.

Highlights:
- Art collections.
- Rotating exhibitions.
- Major Houston museum.
- Free Thursday admission.

Houston Museum of Natural Science and Cockrell Butterfly Center
The Houston Museum of Natural Science is a strong option for visitors interested in prehistoric animals, dinosaurs, gems, and natural science exhibits.

The Cockrell Butterfly Center is connected to the museum but has a separate entrance. It is described as a warm, green environment where butterflies fly freely around visitors.

Admission information from the source:
- Butterfly Center tickets start at $15 for adults and $12 for kids.
- Museum of Natural Science admission starts at $25 for adults and $16 for children.

Highlights:
- Prehistoric animals.
- Dinosaur exhibits.
- Precious gems.
- Butterfly garden.
- Warm indoor green space with free-roaming butterflies.

Discovery Green
Discovery Green is an expansive park area that hosts events and activities. Visitors can bike, play in the grass, relax under trees, sit near the small lake, and ice skate during winter months.

Nearby features include large sculptures and a convention center that hosts concerts and similar events.

Highlights:
- Urban park.
- Events and activities.
- Biking.
- Relaxing green space.
- Small lake.
- Winter ice skating.
- Nearby sculptures and convention center.

Gerald D. Hines Waterwall Park
Gerald D. Hines Waterwall Park features a 64-foot semi-circular fountain that circulates 11,000 gallons of water per minute. It is described as an amazing sight and a strong photo opportunity.

Highlights:
- 64-foot water wall.
- Semi-circular fountain.
- Uptown Houston landmark.
- Photo opportunity.
- Refreshing mist during hot summer months.

The Galleria
The Galleria is Texas’ largest shopping mall and includes more than 400 stores, with more than 70 stores exclusive to Houston.

Highlights:
- Largest mall in Texas.
- More than 400 stores.
- More than 70 stores exclusive to Houston.
- Major shopping destination.

Houston Murals
Houston is known for vibrant street art and large murals. Many murals are located throughout the Arts District and downtown.

Travel tip from the source:
- Search “Houston Murals” online to find maps and addresses for some of the most famous murals in the city.

Highlights:
- Street art.
- Large murals.
- Arts District locations.
- Downtown Houston photo spots.

Spa World Houston
Spa World Houston is a Korean bath house focused on relaxation and wellbeing. It includes baths, saunas, heat and cold therapy rooms, nap areas, Korean meals, and mini golf.

Admission information from the source:
- Day pass is $45 on weekdays.
- Day pass is $55 on weekends and holidays.
- Services such as massage, body scrub, food, and drinks cost extra.

Travel note from the source:
- Guests must fully undress to use the gender-split bath house facilities.
- Visitors who are uncomfortable with that can remain in the communal areas with heat and cold therapy rooms.

Highlights:
- Korean bath house.
- Saunas.
- Baths.
- Heat and cold therapy rooms.
- Nap areas.
- Korean food.
- Mini golf.
- Relaxing cultural experience.

Myx Blend Bar
Myx Blend Bar has locations in Dallas and Houston and specializes in lip cosmetics. Visitors choose a package and design their own lip product from start to finish.

Pricing information from the source:
- Packages start at $55 each.

Highlights:
- Custom lip product activity.
- Glass of champagne included at the start.
- Visitors name and take home their creations.
- Fun group activity.

Travel Tip
For large cities like Houston, CityPASS can be useful. The coupon book allows admission to top tourist attractions and is often cheaper than buying single tickets for each attraction separately.

Where To Eat in Houston

BB’s Tex-Orleans
BB’s Tex-Orleans serves New Orleans-style Cajun cuisine with a Texas twist. The restaurant is known for crab boils and crawfish.

Highlights:
- Tex-Orleans cuisine.
- Crab boils.
- Crawfish.
- Drinks.

The Breakfast Klub
The Breakfast Klub is known nationwide for chicken and waffles and comfort food. There is often a line wrapped around the building. Inside, the walls are covered with paintings of Black musicians and artists.

Popular items:
- Chicken and waffles.
- Fried chicken.
- Chikin Koating.
- Koffee grounds.
- Waffle and Pankake mix.

Highlights:
- Comfort food.
- Chicken and waffles.
- Fried chicken.
- Black musician and artist artwork.
- Strong brunch atmosphere.

Phil & Derek’s
Phil & Derek’s is described as having strong ambiance and atmosphere. It is part jazz lounge, with music filling the space and a piano in the restaurant. Guests can text song requests to a number shown on TV screens.

Popular items:
- Hot chicken and pancakes.

Highlights:
- Brunch vibes.
- Jazz lounge atmosphere.
- Music.
- Piano.
- Song requests.
- Hot chicken and pancakes.

Lost and Found Midtown
Lost and Found Midtown is a good place for breakfast, brunch, or dinner. The Lobster Cajun Pasta is described as very good and worth the higher price. The restaurant is also known for $5 happy hour daiquiris that are sweet but strong.

Popular items:
- Lobster Cajun Pasta.
- Happy hour daiquiris.

Highlights:
- Breakfast, brunch, and dinner.
- Cajun pasta.
- Happy hour drinks.
- Midtown location.

Blue Nile Ethiopian Restaurant

Nightlife and Bars in Houston

Washington Ave
Washington Ave, especially the section from Patterson Street to Westcott Street, is a good area for bar hopping after dinner or pre-gaming with friends.

Bars and nightlife spots mentioned:
- Clutch.
- Sugar Room.
- Bottled Blonde.

Travel note from the source:
- Parking can be limited unless visitors pay for self-parking lots.

Highlights:
- Bar hopping.
- Sports bars.
- Clubs.
- Nightlife district.

Prospect Park
Prospect Park is part sports bar, part live music venue, and part lounge. It is a good place to start a night out. The venue includes food, hookah, music, and a drink menu.

Popular drink:
- Hole in One frozen margarita with strawberry puree and Chandon Rosé.

Highlights:
- Sports bar.
- Live music venue.
- Lounge.
- Food.
- Hookah.
- Large frozen margarita.

Bar 5015
Bar 5015 has indoor and outdoor seating, food, and frozen daiquiris. It is described as grown and laid back, with an age range around 25 and up.

Highlights:
- Food.
- Frozen daiquiris.
- Indoor seating.
- Outdoor seating.
- Mature laid-back atmosphere.

FAO HTX
FAO HTX is a bar with games for groups. It includes ping pong, Jenga, arcade games, and Street Fighter. It is also near other Midtown bars, making it a good starting point for bar hopping.

Highlights:
- Group games.
- Ping pong.
- Jenga.
- Arcade games.
- Street Fighter.
- Midtown bar-hopping location.

13 Celsius
13 Celsius is an intimate wine bar in Midtown. It is described as warm and suitable for date nights, wine lovers, or relaxed nights with friends. The menu includes wines to sample and small plates.

Highlights:
- Wine bar.
- Intimate setting.
- Date night option.
