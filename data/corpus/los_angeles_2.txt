Los Angeles Travel Guide: Things To Do, Neighborhoods, Beaches, and Day Trips

Overview
Los Angeles is best explored as a city of neighborhoods. Each area has its own attractions, restaurants, history, beaches, art, nightlife, and outdoor activities. Instead of trying to see the whole city at once, visitors should plan by neighborhood to reduce travel time and make each day more manageable.

Downtown Los Angeles
Downtown Los Angeles combines old and new landmarks, historic architecture, food markets, cultural attractions, and walkable areas.

Olvera Street
Olvera Street is one of the historic areas of Los Angeles. It includes the Avila Adobe, the oldest standing house in Los Angeles, along with Mexican shops, restaurants, dresses, maracas, and guayaberas. It is a good stop for visitors interested in Los Angeles history and Mexican cultural influence.

Union Station
Union Station is located across from Olvera Street. It is a grand Art Deco train station that still operates today. Visitors can stop by to admire the architecture or use it as a transportation hub.

Chinatown
Chinatown is located a short distance from Olvera Street. Although the area is smaller today, its entrance gates and colorful streets make it a good place for photos and a short walk.

Phillippe The Original
Phillippe The Original is located near this downtown area and is known for French dip sandwiches.

San Antonio Winery
San Antonio Winery is the only remaining winery in downtown Los Angeles. It is more than 100 years old and offers tastings and a restaurant.

Suggested time to visit:
- Olvera Street and Union Station: 1 to 2 hours, depending on shopping and exploring.
- Chinatown: 30 minutes to 1 hour.
- Olvera Street, Union Station, and Chinatown together: about 2 to 3 hours.

Broadway Theater District
Broadway Street was once the center of theater openings and Hollywood-style glamour in Los Angeles. It is known for historic theaters and Art Deco architecture. Visitors interested in architecture and historic neighborhoods can walk down Broadway Street or take a tour with the Los Angeles Conservancy, which may include access inside some of the old theater buildings.

Suggested time to visit:
- Broadway Street walk: about 1 hour.
- Broadway Theater District tour: longer if taking a guided tour.

Bradbury Building
The Bradbury Building was built in 1893 and is the oldest standing commercial building in Los Angeles. Its exterior is simple, but the interior includes Victorian and Art Nouveau architectural details. It is also known for appearing in films such as Blade Runner. Public access is generally limited to the first floor.

Grand Central Market
Grand Central Market is located across from the Bradbury Building. It opened in 1917 and is the oldest market in downtown Los Angeles. Today it is a major food destination featuring many types of California cuisine and vendors that reflect the city’s diversity. Roast To Go is one of the oldest vendors at the market.

Angel’s Flight
Angel’s Flight is a historic funicular located on Bunker Hill near Grand Central Market. It is more than 100 years old and can be combined with a visit to the Bradbury Building and Grand Central Market.

Suggested time to visit:
- Bradbury Building and Grand Central Market: about 1 hour.
- Add extra time if eating at the market or riding Angel’s Flight.

The Last Bookstore
The Last Bookstore is a large independent bookstore in downtown Los Angeles. It sells new and used books, records, tapes, and gifts. It is also known for unique interior spaces and photo opportunities.

Suggested time to visit:
- The Last Bookstore: 1 to 2 hours.
- Allow more time if browsing deeply.

Hollywood and Fairfax Area

Original Farmers Market
The Original Farmers Market at 3rd and Fairfax is an open-air market with grocery stalls, specialty shops, and restaurants. Food options include many cuisines, including sushi and Cajun gumbo. Visitors should make sure they are going to the Farmers Market at 3rd and Fairfax because there are other farmers markets in Los Angeles.

The Grove
The Grove is adjacent to the Original Farmers Market. It is an outdoor shopping and dining area with upscale stores, restaurants, and an old-school trolley that is good for photos. A visit to The Grove pairs well with the Farmers Market.

Suggested time to visit:
- Original Farmers Market and The Grove: at least 2 hours.
- Allow more time if shopping or dining.

Hollywood Boulevard and TCL Chinese Theatre
Hollywood Boulevard is one of the most iconic attractions in Los Angeles. Visitors can see the Hollywood Walk of Fame and the TCL Chinese Theatre. The Chinese Theatre is known for its architecture and its courtyard with celebrity handprints and footprints in concrete. Guided tours of the theater may also be available.

Hollywood Forever Cemetery
Hollywood Forever Cemetery is a historic cemetery and cultural space. It hosts cultural events, outdoor movies, and literary events. Visitors come to see the graves of major entertainment figures, including Mickey Rooney, Douglas Fairbanks, Cecil B. DeMille, and Judy Garland.

Suggested time to visit:
- Hollywood Boulevard, TCL Chinese Theatre, and Hollywood Forever Cemetery: about a half day.

Silver Lake
Silver Lake is a trendy neighborhood known for dining, nightlife, bohemian shops, and the Secret Stairways of Los Angeles. The Music Box Steps, made famous by the Laurel and Hardy film The Music Box, are located between 917 and 935 N. Vendome Street. The Micheltorena Stairs, also known as Stair Candy, are near the intersection of Sunset Boulevard and Santa Monica Boulevard.

Silver Lake can be combined with Hollywood, Griffith Observatory, or downtown Los Angeles. It is a good area for visitors who want a more local neighborhood experience with food, bars, shops, and walking.

Suggested time to visit:
- Silver Lake stairs and neighborhood exploring: 1 to 3 hours.
- Add time for dining, shopping, or nightlife.

West Los Angeles
West Los Angeles includes Beverly Hills, Santa Monica, Bel Air, Brentwood, and other well-known neighborhoods. This area includes museums, beaches, shopping, scenic views, and upscale attractions.

The Getty Center
The Getty Center is located on a hillside in the Santa Monica Mountains and offers panoramic views of Los Angeles. On clear days, visitors may see the Pacific Ocean. The Getty Center is known for its art collection, special exhibitions, gardens, architecture, and white travertine buildings designed by Richard Meier.

The permanent collection includes pre-20th-century art and sculpture, including works such as Vincent Van Gogh’s Irises and a self-portrait of Rembrandt laughing. The Getty Center also has rotating photo exhibitions and special exhibitions.

Admission to the Getty Center is free, but parking requires a fee. The Getty Center is different from the Getty Villa in Malibu.

Suggested time to visit:
- The Getty Center: at least a half day.

The Apple Pan
The Apple Pan is a long-running restaurant known for its hickory burger and pie. It can be visited after the Getty Center.

Rodeo Drive and Beverly Hills
Rodeo Drive and Beverly Hills are known for luxury shopping, expensive real estate, celebrity culture, and sightseeing. This area is a popular tourist stop and can be visited once for the experience, even if visitors do not plan to shop.

Suggested time to visit:
- Rodeo Drive and Beverly Hills: 1 to 2 hours for sightseeing.
- Allow more time for shopping, dining, or guided tours.

Griffith Observatory and Griffith Park
Griffith Observatory is one of the most popular attractions in Los Angeles. It offers city views, planetarium shows, and nighttime views of Los Angeles. It is also a strong option for sunset views and scenic photography.

Griffith Park covers more than 3,000 acres and is one of the most popular hiking areas in Los Angeles County. The park contains hundreds of miles of trails for hiking, biking, and horseback riding. It also offers views of the Hollywood Sign.

Visitors should plan extra time because parking near the observatory can take a while. One option is to park near the Greek Theatre and walk up to the observatory. There is also parking near trailheads for hiking.

Suggested time to visit:
- Griffith Observatory only: 2 to 3 hours.
- Griffith Observatory plus hiking in Griffith Park: half day or longer.

Universal Studios Hollywood
Universal Studios Hollywood is one of the major attractions in Los Angeles and focuses on the film industry, movie-themed attractions, studio experiences, and entertainment. It is a good option for visitors interested in movies, theme parks, or family-friendly attractions.

Suggested time to visit:
- Universal Studios Hollywood: one full day.

La Brea Tar Pits
The La Brea Tar Pits are an active Ice Age fossil excavation site in an urban area. The site includes outdoor areas, a museum, and film exhibits. It is a good attraction for visitors interested in geology, science, dinosaurs, paleontology, history, or family-friendly educational activities.

The La Brea Tar Pits can be combined with Beverly Hills, Rodeo Drive, Miracle Mile, Hollywood Boulevard, or The Grove depending on the visitor’s schedule.

Suggested time to visit:
- La Brea Tar Pits: at least a half day.
- Make it a full day if combining with lunch and nearby attractions.

Los Angeles Beaches
Los Angeles has several beaches that are good for relaxing, walking, surfing, tide pools, and scenic views.

Malibu
Malibu is a strong choice for scenic beaches near Los Angeles. Visitors can explore tide pools, watch surfers, swim, sunbathe, or enjoy coastal scenery. Malibu beaches are often located near expensive homes and scenic hillsides.

Zuma Beach
Zuma Beach is a wide sandy beach in Malibu. It is a good option for visitors who want a classic beach experience with more open sand.

Leo Carrillo State Beach
Leo Carrillo State Beach is known for tide pools and sandy areas. It also has a dog-friendly area on the north end.

Topanga Beach
Topanga Beach is good for surfing and walking. It is close to Los Angeles and has parking where Topanga Canyon Boulevard meets the Pacific Coast Highway.

Suggested time to visit:
- Any single beach: 2 to 4 hours.
- Malibu beach day: half day or full day.
- Tide pool exploration at Leo Carrillo: 1 to 3 hours depending on conditions.

Santa Monica Pier
Santa Monica Pier is one of the classic Los Angeles coastal attractions. It offers ocean views, rides, food, and a lively beach atmosphere. It can be combined with a beach walk, shopping, or dining in Santa Monica.

Suggested time to visit:
- Santa Monica Pier and beach area: 2 to 4 hours.
- Longer if dining, shopping, or spending time on the beach.

Los Angeles Planning Tips
Los Angeles is spread out, so visitors should group activities by neighborhood or area. Instead of planning many attractions across the city in one day, choose two or three nearby activities. This reduces time spent in traffic and makes the trip more enjoyable.

Good combinations include:
- Olvera Street, Union Station, Chinatown, Bradbury Building, and Grand Central Market.
- Farmers Market, The Grove, La Brea Tar Pits, and Hollywood Boulevard.
- Getty Center, Beverly Hills, and Rodeo Drive.
- Griffith Observatory, Griffith Park, Hollywood, and Silver Lake.
- Santa Monica Pier, Malibu, Zuma Beach, or Topanga Beach.

For outdoor activities, consider Griffith Park, Griffith Observatory, Malibu beaches, Zuma Beach, Leo Carrillo State Beach, Topanga Beach, Santa Monica Pier, and the Silver Lake stairways.

For family-friendly activities, consider Universal Studios Hollywood, La Brea Tar Pits, Griffith Observatory, Griffith Park, Santa Monica Pier, the Farmers Market, The Grove, and beach areas.

For food-focused activities, consider Grand Central Market, the Original Farmers Market, Phillippe The Original, San Antonio Winery, and The Apple Pan.
